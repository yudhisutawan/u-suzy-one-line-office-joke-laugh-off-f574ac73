# Candidates: One-Line Office Joke Laugh-Off

## Candidate 1
**Entry:** Our meetings are like a seance—we spend an hour trying to contact people who don't want to talk.
**Characters:** 93
**Reason:** A tight and accurate metaphor capturing the frustration of modern corporate meetings.

## Candidate 2
**Entry:** Calling us a "work family" is accurate because I didn’t choose these people and I’m just waiting for the inheritance.
**Characters:** 117
**Reason:** Subverts the cliché of the "work family" with a dark, surprising punchline.

## Candidate 3
**Entry:** Performance reviews are the corporate equivalent of your cat bringing you a dead bird: you worked hard on it, but nobody actually wanted it.
**Characters:** 140
**Reason:** Uses a strong and fresh visual image to describe the futility of the evaluation process.

## Candidate 4
**Entry:** I told IT my computer was frozen, so they told me to "give it a minute," which is also how my career is currently being handled.
**Characters:** 129
**Reason:** A double punchline linking trivial tech support issues with instant career despair.

## Candidate 5
**Entry:** Being the "go-to person" at work is just a polite way of saying you’re the only one who didn't figure out how to look busy during the reorganization.
**Characters:** 154
**Reason:** Sharp observation of office politics and survival mechanisms in the corporate environment.

## Candidate 6
**Entry:** I bring the energy of a Victorian orphan to every 9 AM, except my "chimney" is a spreadsheet and the "gruel" is lukewarm Nespresso.
**Characters:** 132
**Reason:** Very fresh with unique diction ("Victorian orphan", "gruel"), providing high comedic contrast.

## Candidate 7
**Entry:** The open-office plan was designed to "foster collaboration," but mostly it just fosters the ability to hear Kevin eat a yogurt from forty feet away.
**Characters:** 148
**Reason:** Attacks office design clichés with specific, highly relatable details guaranteed to get a laugh.

## Candidate 8
**Entry:** My boss asked for "honest feedback" in my exit interview, which is like a shark asking the seal how the water tastes right before the bite.
**Characters:** 138
**Reason:** Aggressive and surprising punchline depicting the power imbalance in the office with a fitting analogy.

## Top 3
1. Candidate 3 — The "dead bird" metaphor is original, sharp, and instantly paints a funny picture the judge has never heard before.
2. Candidate 1 — Extremely tight and the "seance" metaphor is a clever observation of modern meeting dynamics.
3. Candidate 7 — Exceptionally strong on relatability with specific details (Kevin & yogurt) that make the joke feel real and instantly funny.
